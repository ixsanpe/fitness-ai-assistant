eval_harness.py · retrieval metrics

What each number is actually measuring

One query, one worked ranking, five metrics computed on it by hand — so precision@k, recall@k, MAP@k, NDCG@k, and MRR stop being five interchangeable "goodness" scores and become five different questions.

Shared example
"barbell exercise for hamstrings"
relevant in corpus: 22 k: 5 hits in top‑5: 3
This query and its 22-item relevant set are real, from data/eval/test_queries.jsonl. The top‑5 ranking below is a hand-picked worked example — not live output from the pipeline — chosen so every metric below produces a different, instructive number.
01 · precision@k

Of what you showed, how much was right?

Only looks inside the top‑k window. Doesn't know or care that 19 other relevant exercises exist outside it.

  1. 1Romanian_Deadlifthit
  2. 2Leg_Press—
  3. 3Good_Morninghit
  4. 4Barbell_Hip_Thrust—
  5. 5Stiff-Legged_Barbell_Deadlifthit
precision@5 = hits in top‑5 ÷ 5 = 3 ÷ 5 = 0.60

Rewards: a clean top‑k window. Blind spot: treats a hit at rank 1 and a hit at rank 5 identically, and is silent on everything the corpus has that never made the list.

02 · recall@k

Of everything relevant, how much did you surface?

Same 3 hits, but the denominator is now the full relevant set — all 22 dots below, only 3 filled.

recall@5 = hits in top‑5 ÷ |relevant| = 3 ÷ 22 = 0.136

Rewards: coverage of the whole relevant set. Blind spot: structurally capped by k — with 22 relevant items and k=5, recall can never exceed 5⁄22 ≈ 0.227, no matter how good the retriever is. A "low" recall@k is often a corpus-size artifact, not a retrieval failure.

03 · map@k

How good was the ranking, not just the hit count?

At every hit, take the precision "so far" up to that rank, then average those snapshots. Reordering the same 3 hits changes this score even though precision@5 stays 0.60.

  1. 1Romanian_DeadliftP@1 = 1/1 = 1.00
  2. 2Leg_Press—
  3. 3Good_MorningP@3 = 2/3 = 0.67
  4. 4Barbell_Hip_Thrust—
  5. 5Stiff-Legged_Barbell_DeadliftP@5 = 3/5 = 0.60
AP@5 = (1.00 + 0.67 + 0.60) ÷ min(|relevant|, 5) = 2.27 ÷ 5 = 0.453

Rewards: getting hits early, not just eventually. Blind spot: a hit that arrives at rank 5 still drags the average down even though it's inside the window — MAP punishes "found it late" harder than precision does.

04 · ndcg@k

Given what you found, was it ordered well?

Each rank is worth less than the one before it (weight = 1 ÷ log₂(rank+1)), then the actual ordering is compared against the best possible ordering of these same 3 hits — not against the full relevant set.

  1. 1Romanian_Deadliftweight 1.00
  2. 2Leg_Pressweight 0.63
  3. 3Good_Morningweight 0.50
  4. 4Barbell_Hip_Thrustweight 0.43
  5. 5Stiff-Legged_Barbell_Deadliftweight 0.39
DCG = 1.00 + 0.50 + 0.39 = 1.89 · IDCG (3 hits at ranks 1–3) = 1.00 + 0.63 + 0.50 = 2.13
NDCG@5 = DCG ÷ IDCG = 1.89 ÷ 2.13 = 0.885

Rewards: front-loading the hits you do find — this is why it scores 0.885 even though precision@5 was only 0.60. Blind spot: with binary relevance (hit / no hit, no "more relevant") it plateaus fast; one well-placed hit can already look strong.

05 · mrr

How fast did the first good answer show up?

Only rank 1's hit matters here — everything after the first hit is thrown away for this query, which is why it's usually averaged (MRR) across many queries rather than read per-query.

  1. 1Romanian_Deadlifthit
  2. 2Leg_Pressignored
  3. 3Good_Morningignored
  4. 4Barbell_Hip_Thrustignored
  5. 5Stiff-Legged_Barbell_Deadliftignored
RR = 1 ÷ (rank of first hit) = 1 ÷ 1 = 1.00

RR is per query. MRR is the mean of RR over the whole eval set — e.g. three queries whose first hit lands at rank 1, 2, and 3:

queryfirst hit rankRR
barbell exercise for hamstrings11 / 1 = 1.000
plyometrics exercise for adductors21 / 2 = 0.500
triceps exercise using machine31 / 3 = 0.333
mean → MRR—0.611

Rewards: speed to a usable answer — the right metric when a system only ever surfaces one result, e.g. a RAG pipeline's top context. Blind spot: a second and third correct hit right below the first add nothing to the score.
